MAILCHIMP AND SPAM

Probably not directly connected, but they share a root cause. The spam pages came from someone getting into the old site's back end, while the fake sign-ups come from bots that find and fill in sign-up forms. Right now, neither the site nor the form has anything to stop automated attacks.

The new site closes the website side. For Mailchimp, two built-in settings stop most of this: a confirmation email for new subscribers, so fake addresses never join your list, and a "verify you're human" check on the form. If those are already on, I'll look more closely at how the spammers are getting through. I'll also clear out the fake contacts already on your list.


SECURITY AND MAINTENANCE

The move itself is the biggest security improvement. The new site has no login page and no add-ons to break into, just finished pages, so there's far less to attack. It will be hosted on Cloudflare, which automatically blocks malicious traffic and lets us monitor it. I'll also set up the accounts so each person has their own login instead of a shared password, with two-step verification turned on.

There's nothing to update or patch each month. After each support period, the main ongoing need is content (new application dates, a new class of graduates, sponsor logos), which I can handle at $40/hour. Most updates take under an hour. Later on, I could build a simple admin dashboard so you can make changes yourselves; that's a larger project that could be a Phase 3.
